Wonder 25: Meteora Monasteries: Explore Greece’s Sky-High Spiritual Marvel, Hotels, and Travel Tips

The Meteora monasteries in Greece are a stunning collection of ancient Eastern Orthodox monasteries built high atop towering rock formations in the Thessaly region. Dating back to the 14th century, these monasteries were constructed by monks seeking solitude and protection from invasions. Today, they stand as UNESCO World Heritage Sites and attract visitors from across the globe who come to experience their breathtaking views, serene atmosphere, and rich history.

Each monastery represents centuries of devotion, architecture, and human perseverance. Surrounded by surreal landscapes, the Meteora monasteries invite travelers to discover Greece’s spiritual heart and cultural heritage from a whole new height.

History of Greece’s Meteora Monasteries

The history of Meteora is a tale of faith and resilience. Monks began settling in the area around the 11th century, but it wasn’t until the 14th century that the first monasteries were built—accessible only by rope ladders and nets. These lofty retreats provided safety from Ottoman invasions and served as centers of art, theology, and education.

Over time, the monasteries endured wars, political upheavals, and natural challenges, yet they continued to inspire spiritual seekers and travelers alike. Today, six of the original twenty-four monasteries remain active, offering visitors a chance to explore sacred frescoes, ancient relics, and a living example of Byzantine devotion and craftsmanship.

Where to stay?

When visiting Meteora, it’s best to stay in nearby towns that offer comfort, accessibility, and scenic views. Here are some excellent areas to consider:

  1. Kalambaka
    1. Kalambaka sits at the foot of the Meteora rock formations and serves as the main gateway to the monasteries. It offers a range of hotels—from cozy guesthouses to luxury stays—many featuring spectacular views of the cliffs. Restaurants, cafes, and transport options make Kalambaka a convenient and lively base.
  2. Kastraki
    1. Just a short distance from Kalambaka, Kastraki offers a quieter and more traditional atmosphere. Surrounded by natural beauty, this charming village provides intimate guesthouses, family-run hotels, and easy access to the hiking paths leading up to the monasteries.

Both Kalambaka and Kastraki offer incredible views, local hospitality, and easy routes to explore the awe-inspiring Meteora monasteries.

Recommendations:

  1. Visit early in the morning or late in the afternoon to avoid crowds and capture stunning photos.
  2. Wear comfortable walking shoes—the terrain can be steep and uneven.
  3. Check the opening hours of each monastery before visiting; some are closed on certain days.
  4. Dress modestly—shoulders and knees should be covered inside the monasteries.
  5. Consider taking a guided tour to learn about Meteora’s spiritual and historical background.
  6. Bring water, especially if hiking between monasteries.
  7. Respect the peaceful atmosphere—it’s a place of worship as well as a tourist site.

Best time to visit Greece

  1. Spring (April to June)
    The weather is mild, flowers are in bloom, and the landscape is vibrant. This is one of the best seasons to explore the monasteries without heavy crowds.
  2. Summer (July to August)
    Expect hot and sunny weather with increased tourist activity. Ideal for those who enjoy lively atmospheres and longer daylight hours.
  3. Fall (September to October)
    Warm days, cool evenings, and fewer visitors make autumn perfect for hiking and sightseeing.
  4. Winter (November to March)
    Cooler temperatures and fewer tourists provide a peaceful atmosphere, though some areas may have limited access due to weather.
